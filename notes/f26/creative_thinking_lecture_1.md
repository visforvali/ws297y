# Reclaim the World: Question Your Teaspoons

Greetings, comrades. I'm Dr. Mani, alias Flame Thief on our Discord server; 20 years of teaching writing and rhetorical studies and disability studies; Siberian cat named Athena; into body modification from piercings to tattoos to scarifications covering up old self-injury scars and used to have a magnetic implant in my finger; I'm openly disabled with chronic pain and fatigue and a few ongoing critical situations that Pace resists accommodating; and I'm fucking tired.

Side note: I don't mandate video but there are times I may ask you to turn your cameras on to showcase a class activity or for help with social cues.

Who in this class is fucking tired? Why? What, right now, are you most tired of?

This class is WS 297Y: Creative Writing and Social Justice, but this semester I've rebranded it as Creative Reclamation, on the assumption we're all fucking tired. Of having shit stolen from us, in ways we're not even meant to notice, day in and day out, by institutions of power that blame us for the theft or force us to give ourselves away and make us believe we did so willingly.

This class emphasizes praxis, or the idea that theory is meaningless until put into practice, so it also emphasizes low "after hours" labor and high in-class participation. Every class session will ideally consist of idea generation, shooting the shit like an extracurricular book club, and some amount of collaborative close reading to recognize what's been taken from you and figure out how to take it back.

We'll do some reading and writing en route to that goal but more on a "just vibes" level than what you've probably come to believe "academic rigor" means.

Before I dive into aspects of the course, the mic is open for questions, comments, or concerns!

- **Course Highlights**: How to read the Calendar; all readings provided for free but you are required to obtain a blank journal for this class only as you may submit it to me at the end of the semester; primary tech platforms are the course website (which has everything you're supposed to do) and Discord (where you're supposed to do it); grading is holistic, course grade is based largely on vocal participation during voice chat and text participation during text chat, alt-grading policy guarantees at least a B+ if you do everything you're asked to do in the spirit you're asked to do it; attendance = participation in this course; don't use AI; embrace uncertainty, not knowing, imperfection, failure, randomness, and strangeness
- **Assignment Overview**: Pretty much everything is completion-based grading until the end of the course (assuming you do what the assignment is asking you to do), all low-stakes writing exercises build to the high-stakes project, called an Affective Atlas, a multimodal feminist cartography of your interiority and reclamation processes over the course of the semester--"an expedition log into your subconscious" guided by our discussions and the readings we'll use to prompt idea generation in our voice chat sessions
- **Readings**: Short excerpts, sometimes on the level of Tumblr or Twitter screenshots, really meant to focus your attention on interesting ideas and get you thinking through your own and how to use the new modes of thinking you develop to reclaim something you've lost
- **Discord**: This is just a place to talk in voice chat and text chat in classroom. Typically we'll have one sync voice chat (for shooting the shit idea generation) and one sync text chat (for refining and adding to those ideas and for collaborative close-reading); select roles right now if you haven't, make sure to choose a pfp and change your display name to an alias and post an introduction by the weekend

With all this talk of reclamation, take 5 minutes and make a list of ways of being, doing, thinking, knowing, and making that you feel have been stolen from you by institutions of power. It's okay if this is a short list right now, as we were never meant to notice these thefts, so you may not be aware right now.
