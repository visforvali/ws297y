# Reclaim the World: Question Your Teaspoons

## 1.1

Greetings, comrades. I'm Dr. Mani, alias Flame Thief on our Discord server; 20 years of teaching writing and rhetorical studies and disability studies; Siberian cat named Athena; into body modification from piercings to tattoos to scarifications covering up old self-injury scars and used to have a magnetic implant in my finger; I'm openly disabled with chronic pain and fatigue and a few ongoing critical situations that Pace resists accommodating; and I'm fucking tired.

Side note: I don't mandate video but there are times I may ask you to turn your cameras on to showcase a class activity or for help with social cues.

Who in this class is fucking tired? Why? What, right now, are you most tired of?

This class is WS 297Y: Creative Writing and Social Justice, but this semester I've rebranded it as Creative Reclamation, on the assumption we're all fucking tired. Of having shit stolen from us, in ways we're not even meant to notice, day in and day out, by institutions of power that blame us for the theft or force us to give ourselves away and make us believe we did so willingly.

This class emphasizes praxis, or the idea that theory is meaningless until put into practice, so it also emphasizes low "after hours" labor and high in-class participation. Every class session will ideally consist of idea generation, shooting the shit like an extracurricular book club, and some amount of collaborative close reading to recognize what's been taken from you and figure out how to take it back.

We'll do some reading and writing en route to that goal but more on a "just vibes" level than what you've probably come to believe "academic rigor" means.

Before I dive into aspects of the course, the mic is open for questions, comments, or concerns!

- **Intro Writing**: For two minutes, write: What comes to mind for you regarding the word "failure"?
	- *My Free-Write*: I'm terrified of failing and fail every day. Disability often means that even things I excel at leave me feeling like a failure because I'm expected to be perfect especially as a South Asian American, a model minority stereotyped to be smart and "on" and supposed to over-perform cognitively and emotionally all the time. I can see on paper I'm profoundly accomplished and I still feel like an impostor. I feel like I deserve very little and deny myself happiness when I fail. I live in deathly fear of what happens when everyone sees me for the impostor I am. It's been 40 years of this BS, and I don't want this for my students, so I struggle to make this visible to them, for us all to make our unwellness visible, because failure shouldn't be framed like this, and we shouldn't orient to it this way.
- **"Approaches to What?"**: Today we try to reclaim how we notice and think about the world, space, society, objects, our habits, the ordinary vs the spectacular--because we're trained to see meaning only in the "big event," the acute eruption, the scandal--not the "infra-ordinary" (like the opposite of "extra-ordinary") or "endotic" (like the opposite of "exotic") which are Perec's terms for the habitual, the common, the essential and yet inadmissible and intolerable, like his example about daily working conditions in the coalmines being more scandalous than a coalmine disaster
- How do you define what's significant? What might be insidiously wrong with that?
- How *are* we to speak of them? To find them and give them meaning when we've been trained for so long to ignore them, when we've habituated their presence as background noise? How do we create a practice of "questioning our teaspoons"?
- What does it mean to notice the *insignificant* as a vehicle for making meaning? How well would this go over at university?
- What would it mean to shift our questioning from the polished, the event and spectacle-driven, to the "trivial and futile," the infra-ordinary, the endotic, that which has ceased to astonish and goes unquestioned?
- What is the last common, infra-ordinary thing that astonished you, that you for whatever reason restored a sense of wonder to?
- Tell me something infra-ordinary about how you spend your time each day, that you're just now noticing, that you might rescue from forgettability
- Tell me something infra-ordinary about a particular rhythm of your college life, that you're just now noticing, that was imposed on you and that you take for granted and that you were meant not to notice, and that came at the cost of something else
- Tell me about an infra-ordinary practice or habit that attending Pace has cultivated in you, that is meant to go unnoticed and certainly not astonish, that has been imposed on you at the expense of an infra-ordinary habit or practice that might regularly astonish or revitalize?
- What's significant about holding space for the mundane and tiny occurrences of the every day? We might begin to notice where impositions and substitutions have cost us our own infra-ordinary habits
