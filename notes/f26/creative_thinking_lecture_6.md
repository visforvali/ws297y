# Reclaim Failure: Failure as Success

## 6.1 Async Text Chat

Welcome back, comrades!

**Housekeeping**: As previously announced on Discord and in class, I'll be dealing with a medical situation starting this week, with an as-of-now uncertain recovery timeline, so we'll be pivoting to async text chat for a couple of weeks (pending new information). I'll likely be delayed in reviewing your work for a while, and I appreciate you bearing with me in advance!

This week, you'll engage in a creative activity that will span the whole week (both Tue and Thu): creating an alphabetic archive of bad feelings. Think of it like a "hacked" dictionary, where each letter stands for a bad feeling that gets redefined through a narrative about your experience of that bad feeling.

This project is inspired by similar work by Ann Cvetkovich, Sara Ahmed, Lauren Berlant, and Karin Michalski. The goal of it (in addition to becoming part of your Affective Atlas project) is to question the relationship between feeling like a failure and compulsory happiness, in order to begin to reclaim both failure and happiness for yourself &mdash; to find self-recognition, validation, and collectivity in in unhappiness. As Ahmed writes, "It is not simply that we recognize that unhappiness is collective or shared; it is also that we realize that challenging happiness can only be a shared project."

So your guiding question for this week as you work on this project is: How do we make feelings we're meant to hide to our own detriment public and political?

First, revisit the readings, class notes, and your notes from class discussions so far; you may also want to reflect on your process of building a bibliographic altar. Thinking back to Perec and the infra-ordinary, for instance: bad feelings can be crisis-level, or medical &mdash; like clinical depression, anxiety, or sickness &mdash; but more often they're everyday negative feelings, like feeling stuck, numb, unfocused, overwhelmed, isolated, unsatisfactory.

Now, sit down with your journal and make a list of at least one such bad feeling for each letter of the alphabet &mdash; trying to ensure that the majority of your words are infra-ordinary &mdash; that you feel have been especially imposed on or amplified by Pace or academic culture. 

Next, choose one word per letter and define each word briefly, considering your own experience of that bad feeling and how you experience it as part of a struggle against compulsory happiness &mdash; the kind of happy we're "supposed" to be. Then, write a brief narrative that tells the story of that definition &mdash; that conveys your experience of that bad feeling.

After listing words and drafting definitions, make a list of 1-3 tangible objects that, for you, further illustrate the word, definition, experience you've just recorded &mdash; even if the connection between the objects and your writing doesn't seem like it would make sense to an outside audience. Identify and briefly describe the object at the end of each dictionary entry.

While this isn't a required reading, <a href="../../assets/pdfs/khanmalek_restrepo_rhodes_decolonial_feminist_epistemology_of_the_bed_2020.pdf" target="_blank">Khanmalek & rhodes' "A Decolonial Feminist Epistemology of the Bed"</a> it might help you think about what a "hacked" dictionary could look like (this one isn't about bad feelings, but it does model the process of choosing and redefining words through definitions and experience).

In the end, your alphabetic archive of bad feelings will be a collection of vernacular, resistive definitions related through storying &mdash; a critical political intervention that you'll include with your final Affective Atlas at the end of the semester!
