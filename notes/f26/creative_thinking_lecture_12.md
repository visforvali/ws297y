# Reclaim What's Wasted: Everything Can Be Used

## 12.1 Voice Chat

Welcome back, comrades!

Leve's *Works*, the book from which this is excerpted, is best described by the first item &mdash; its a catalogue describing works conceived of but not realized by its author, each of them then having compatible and incongruous qualities &mdash; a sense of failure, humor, or wonder; a wish or wistfulness; an irreverence; a dreaminess; a profound solemnity. Some of the items described here, such as the ones that describe art installations, are a little more grounded and "literary" than others. Some are short, punchy, almost funny. 

The book contains 533 of these works &mdash; it's less a catalogue of actual works than of failures or of disappointing half-starts but rendered joyously. *Works* is an exercise in making the familiar strange, resituating familiar objects, images, and experiences and turning them into something novel through substitutions, replacements, reversals, erasures, and variations on items that appeared earlier in the text. 

What substitutions, replacements, reversals, erasures, variations on items that come before stuck out to you most and why?

What do you think they communicate about "failed works" or the process or value of recording them?

Would you describe this as a book of failure? Or of potential?

How do these items help you reconceive of unfinished or impossible, weird work as potential rather than failure?

What do our failures and unrealized potentials say about us that *isn't* about our self-worth as measured by productivity: i.e., what kind of (auto)biography might be found in such a catalogue?

What does this catalogue suggest about what we should value in art and the very process of writing and creation?

Now, time permitting, let's try to realize some of these items to see what it feels like to work with unrealized (or unrealizable) potential!

- Draw the world from memory on a blank sheet of paper... but draw it without lifting your pen. Then reflect on where borders become necessarily altered or connected. Where you had to draw lines crossing water or land, do you perceive those lines as bridges or as refugee lines of flight? Why did you choose those points to draw those lines?
- Write an invitation to an event you wanted to do but never took place to later be framed in your Affective Atlas journal.
- Take a macro selfie of your eye and whatever is reflected in it. How does the resulting photograph make you see your eye (and your vision and how and what you see) differently?
- What building at Pace would you transform into a cemetery? If the rooms are then individual graves, who or what would be buried in them?

Keep these activities for inclusion with your final Affective Atlas project!
