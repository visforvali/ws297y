# Reclaim Thinking II: Thought Without Image

## 3.1 Voice Chat

Welcome back, comrades!

**Housekeeping**: Modalities and scheduling changes have been updated on the Calendar. As a side note, it might be better to wait to post your Affective Atlas Entries until closer to the deadline or at least after we've discussed a text somewhat thoroughly, as I evaluate them against the content of the texts, close-reading at the level of wording, and our idea-generating discussions.

To recap from our previous disscussions: Deviating from the dogmatic image of thought about scholarly writing opens up new possibilities for thinking and academic writing. The dogmatic image of thought refers to the subjective presuppositions that structure how we think and stifle creative, conceptual, actually natural thought &mdash; thinking without image by contrast is a form of thinking that must be created, after first destroying the dogmatic image of thought. Thought might function when we recognize an object or something that already is graspable for thought but according to Deleuze we aren't really thinking; we only start thinking when an encounter with something "unrecognizable" destroys your dogmatic image of thought and forces us to creatively think. By extension writing can be discussed the same way &mdash; i.e. the idea that similarly "everyone knows," as though it were common sense, what it means to do academic writing or qualitative research writing. 

Today, we're going to think a little bit about the conditions under which the dogmatic image of thought might be best destroyed. 

Consider the top 1-2 "subjective presuppositions" that together make up the dogmatic image of thought that has restricted how you think &mdash; the ones most in need of destruction so that you can create a thought without image in its place.

Where do these presuppositions come from? In other words, where do you think you internalized them, acquired them, where might they have originated from well before being imposed on you (e.g., ideas about academic writing might come from the education system or home).

Importantly in the course of our discussion, we talked a lot about the university's contributions to the image of thought: respectability politics, sycophancy, adherence to power dynamics, 

We'll go over these in more detail in Thursday's text chat, but other scholars have suggested trying things like the following to create a thought without image:

- Fearlessly question things, be unorthodox and transgressive, and ask provocative questions
- Avoid the impulse to self-censor your radical extreme, and seemingly absurd ideas when thinking and writing and engaging in other aspects of inquiry
- Actively experiment and see where it takes your research and writing
- Be courageous in pursuing new directions in your research; take risks and become more comfortable with taking risks
- Reject the dogmatic image of thought in general and in qualitative research and writing in particular
- Value your creativity and engage in forms of thinking, research, and writing that encourage and strengthen your creativity
- When thinking, researching, and writing, view "stuck places" as unique opportunities to produce something new instead of something to be solved through existing practices or techniques

Let's try close-reading the following lines!
