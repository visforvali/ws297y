# Reclaim Survival: Surviving and Thriving

## 13.1 Voice Chat

Today's async text chat will also give you space and time to build your own feminist survival kit!

First, some context for this excerpt:

Ahmed describes the need for and process of building a feminist survival kit, survival itself being an act of protest, and how we care for ourselves being an expression of feminist care. The point of the kit is the items in the kit but also the kit itself: knowing that you have a space that serves as a repository for what you need to survive. As Ahmed notes, "Survival here refers not only to living on, but to keeping going in the more profound sense of keeping going with one's commitments." 

Consider the following questions in #🙋classroom:

- What is the feminist killjoy? What is the work of the feminist killjoy?
- What do you stockpile for yourself for the times when things get really hard? To support your task of being the feminist killjoy?
- What makes these survival kits feminist, antiracist, anti-ableist?

According to Ahmed, "Survival can also be about keeping one's hopes alive; holding on to the projects that are projects insofar as they have yet to be realized. You might have to become willful to hold on when you are asked to let go; to let it go. Survival can thus be what we do for others, with others. We need each other to survive; we need to be part of each other's survival" (235).

Ahmed builds her feminist survival kit around the idea that "feminism needs feminists to survive" and the idea that there is a place to deposit what we need to survive. She includes these 10 categories in her kit: books, things, tools, time, life, permission notes, killjoys, humor, feelings, bodies. 

Armed with these categories and how she (re)defines them, assemble your own feminist survival kit. No need to post your kits this week! This activity is designed to extend into your Affective Atlas entry for the week, which is due on Tuesday after fall break (so you can work on it before/after the break, or during the break, as you prefer).

Use the following questions to guide you as you assemble your feminist survival kits and write your Affective Atlas entries:

- What would you include in your survival kit under each category? That is, what books, things, tools, time, life, permission notes, killjoys, humor, feelings, and bodies belong in your feminist survival kit, and why? (In addition to writing about these items, photograph any tangible objects that you'd include in your survival kit.)
- What acts of reclamation over the course of the semester feel especially important to include in your survival kit, and why?
- What practical solutions have you developed or enacted over the course of the semester that feel important to include in your survival kit, and why?
