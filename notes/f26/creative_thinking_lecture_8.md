# Reclaim Your Time: Work Vs. Labor



The Manual Labours collective produces research documents in the form of zines and publications as a practice-based research project with the aim of reconsidering temporalities of work and to reassert the physical, i.e. manual, aspects of immaterial, affective, emotional labor
- *Non-Stop Inertia* is on their reading list; the excerpts we read focus on contemporary precarity, jobseeking, and how to get out of these time structures of work
- So today we begin to reclaim these time structures of work, meaning today we begin to identify what they are, where and how they work on us, and how to hijack them for our own ends
- When does work start? When does work end?
- Some context for each piece:
- *Precarity 2.0*: Daily life has become precarious and we have to live in a state of perpetual readiness where little to nothing is stable or predictable, from routines to predictable occupations and income--and feeling saturated in anxiety and anguish that either has concrete objects like job termination or no object at all; as Virno says, "It is the relationship with the world as a whole as a source of danger"--and the precariat is expected to hide these feelings that may come upon you at a moment's notice
- *Jobseeking*: Jobseeking itself has become a job in which the jobs yield ever more intermittent and precarious work, in this career jobseekers look busy and sell themselves and brand and rebrand and endlessly jobseek on the job---jobseeking has become a worldview in place of worldviews cultivated in stable workplaces---CV-grooming becomes a consequence of this, a manicured autobiography, emotional labor that's a micro-management of feeling and of self where the self is a series of smooth continuous accomplishments that add up to a characterless individual---an ongoing never finished project like jobseeking itself; we've moved from valuing craft to valuing stagecraft, costuming and adapting on the fly for whatever workplace will have us
- Southwood asks: "What are we not thinking about during all those hours of jobseeking, networking, and CV-building? What interests, worries, and fantasies might we otherwise have? What kind of dangerous spaces might open up, in what kind of jeopardy might we put ourselves and this dynamic system if we resigned from our jobs as jobseekers?"
- *Ways Out*: "I would prefer not to" can't work in a temp work economy because the ability for workers to directly confront employers has been bypassed
- "How do we articulate negation in a culture from which negativity itself has been banished?"
- What has your experience of jobseeking been?
- How would you describe your process of CV-grooming?
- What's the effect of having to exist in a state of constant readiness?
- What's the effect of precarity, the constant fear, anguish, and feelings of sudden existential vulnerability?
- When/where do you notice these impositions and their impacts most?
- What's the effect of being expected to hide those feelings? What's the effect of taken-for-granted stress and concrete dangers like losing a job as well as the feeling of precarity--and the burdens required of you to avoid it--and what are the burdens you have had to take on to try to stave off precarity?

## 6.2 Text Chat

- Today's class is sync chat in which we'll share lines and passages that resonated with you, confused you, or that you'd otherwise like to engage with more deeply
- **Guiding Discussion Questions**:
	- From close-reading the words being used in these passages, what do you think the passage means?
- **Lines I Chose**:
	- Daily life becomes precarious. Planning ahead becomes difficult, routines are impossible to establish. Work, of whatever sort, might begin or end anywhere at a moment's notice, and the burden is always on the worker to create the next opportunity and to surf between roles.
	- Feelings of sudden existential vulnerability now come upon the individual as if from nowhere, in the midst of indifference, in the banal space of work
	- The precarious worker is then saddled with an additional duty: to hide these feelings.
	- Virno: Fear for a determinate reason was something socially governable while anguish over precarity, over finitude, was something that religions or philosophy tried to administer. Now, by contrast, with globalisation these two elements become one.
	- Meanwhile for many employees the old-fashioned purposeful social activity of work has been replaced by a similarly privatised and professionalised form of jobseeking-on-the-job, a vocation which is attended to not only in the act of "looking busy" and selling oneself to the boss during work hours, but also by scrolling through vacancies and filling in applications during downtime and the parenthetical hours of commuting
	- Jobseeking has become something abstract, an entire worldview such as used to be provided by the structure of work itself.
	- The CV is a particular sub-genre of post-Fordist autobiography, a copy-and-paste cosmetic narrative which accentuates the positives and papers over any cracks; ironically, at a time when continuity in work is at its weakest, one's life history must be made to seem as smooth and characterless as a shampoo advertisement. This again is a form of emotional labour, a micro-management of feeling.
	- agencies promise utter detachment from the content of work, which appears to exist in a social vacuum, and at the same time demand absolute positive engagement with its forn1, its language of individual aspiration and presentation.
	- The old skills of craftsmanship or technical proficiency are now secondary, even in manual occupations, to the new skill of linguistic and semiotic virtuosity, a kind of stagecraft: being able to adapt to whatever environment and identity one is thrown into and improvise a role around its unwritten rules of costume, gesture and language.
