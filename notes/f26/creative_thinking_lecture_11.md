# Reclaim Refusal: The Art of Saying No

## 11.1 Voice Chat

Welcome back, comrades!

This excerpt is from *No!*, a treatise and guidebook on the use of complaint and refusal as a means of resistance against institutional power, given how institutions create toxic hostile environments that stigmatize complainers and make complaint dangerous &mdash; but "no" is also how we clarify our projects and priorities and find our people.

Complaints are given a negative valence but are really information-sharing mechanisms intended to create better and safer spaces for all of us. There are many ways of standing in solidarity with the precariat &mdash; or if you're part of the precariat, there are creative, self-protective ways of complaining and saying no to power. For this reason as well, Ahmed suggests we need *more* complainers: complaint collectives, or systems that remind complainers they aren't alone. Because Complainers are rarely alone. 

What does it mean to say no to an institution or institutional prerogative? When in the university setting as students can you say no?

What is a recent or long-term ongoing complaint you have about Pace, whether you've registered it officially, shared it informally, or stayed silent? 

What is a complaint you've never spoken about Pace that you'd like to share now?

Ahmed reminds us that we're "supposed" to become part of institutional culture, which involves protecting it from complaint and by complaints designed to defend it (by suppressing valid, community-oriented, anti-institutional complaints) until you become a yea-sayer automatically &mdash; because saying "yes" no matter what means you're more likely to advance in your career or gain other material incentives, whatever harms you perpetrate. 

People are rewarded for sacrificing their principles.

So according to Ahmed, the question isn't who can afford to fight for change (since so many who qualify won't) but how can we fight for change?

What does it mean or look like to be a killjoy at Pace, in the classroom or administrative setting?

**Close-Reading Ahmed**:

Let's try close-reading the following lines! From close-reading the words being used in the lines below, what do you think each excerpt means?

- "You might recognize yourself in someone else's complaint or you might recognize an institution."
- "Complaints, once made, have their own lives. But their lives remain entangled with ours."
- "The negativity of a complaint is directed outward: It is how we say no to an existing state of affairs or refuse to accept things as they are. But when you become a complainer, negativity is assumed to come from within."
- "Claiming the name feminist killjoy means being willing to be heard as a complainer, heard as negative for not wanting to reproduce the same things, heard as destructive for the same reason. To complain is to be a killjoy at work."
- "To be heard as complaining is not to be heard."
- "Becoming part of an institution can reference the time it takes to say yes to its demands, until it becomes almost automatic to do so. That's why, when complaints teach us about the nature of institutions, they are teaching us how social forms persist over time: Those who say yes are more likely to progress."
- "To build a new world we need to dismantle the existing one. In saying no, or by saying it, we search for an alternative. We complain for a more just world. That for matters. We are more used to speaking of complaining about something or against it. Complaining for means we have to work against something to bring something else about."
- "Our work becomes to cause a crisis in the system."
